## What Happened With the Anthropic IPO

So first let me give you the timeline. Anthropic made its S-1 public on 26 October, then on 10 November it started the roadshow and told everyone the range, $70 to $78 for 825 million shares. Then on 18 November the price was set at $83 and today the trading starts. One thing to know is that there is no fixed time for the first trade. The market opens at 9:30 in the morning US Eastern time but the first trade of a new stock can come hours after that, because Nasdaq waits till the lead bank says it is ready. So don't sit there refreshing at 9:30 and think something is broken.

## Anthropic Valuation: Why Every Number Is Different

Now the valuation, and over here every website is saying a different number. At the range, the numbers were between $1.25 trillion and $1.45 trillion. A lot of the coverage before pricing was saying around $1.4 trillion, and some earlier reports talked about $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion which is not what we are looking at now. How is it so? Because it depends on which shares you count, and once the stock is trading the number will change every single day anyway. So until you see the market cap on a real quote page, treat any trillion dollar number as an estimate.

## Can You Buy Anthropic Stock at the $83 IPO Price?

Okay, now the main question. Can you buy at $83? Most people can not. Anthropic kept somewhere around 15 to 20 percent of the shares for regular investors and that went through brokers like Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab, SoFi and E\*TRADE, and with six times more orders than shares a lot of people who asked would get nothing or really small amounts. Everybody else buys on the open market after the trading starts, at whatever the market price is. That price can be higher than $83 or lower, and the opening price comes from the buy and sell orders and not from the IPO price.

## How to Buy ANT Stock on Day One

So if you want to buy ANT today you need a brokerage account which offers Nasdaq stocks. Search for ANT, check that the company name says Anthropic, and then place your order. Be really careful over here, a new stock moves very fast on the first day, so the price you see can be different from the price you get, and that is why some guides say to wait a few days and see how it settles. And one more thing, if anybody is offering you Anthropic shares outside a proper broker, like on a random website or in a message on social media, stay away from it. Anthropic itself has warned about unauthorized stock sales.

## Buying Anthropic Stock From India and Other Countries

Now if you are outside the US, like in India, this part is for you. India is not on the list of countries where Anthropic ran its international retail offering, so there is no official way to apply for the IPO price from India. What Indian investors can do is buy after the listing, through a platform which gives access to US stocks, and the money goes out through the RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme which has a limit of $250,000 a year. There are tax things also like TCS, which is reported to be 20 percent on the amount above 10 lakh rupees, so check the current rules with your platform or a tax person before you send any money. Other countries are different, in Canada for example Wealthsimple was one of the selling agents, so see what your own broker offers.

## Why Most People Pay More Than $83

Now let me explain the logic behind this and why I said that most people will pay more than $83. Why? Because the IPO price is only for the people who got an allocation, and with six times more orders than shares, the rest of the demand moves to the open market on day one. And when a lot of people want the same thing and there is not enough of it, the price can go up, and it can swing down just as quickly, you know. SpaceX is a good example, its stock has gone up and down a lot since its June IPO and a financial planner quoted by NerdWallet said to expect a similar ride with Anthropic. Also Anthropic is not profitable yet, it lost around $104 billion on paper in the first nine months of this year but NerdWallet says most of that is accounting charges and not real cash going out, though its revenue is growing really fast. So should you care? As a tech person, yes, because now everyone can see the real numbers in a public filing.
